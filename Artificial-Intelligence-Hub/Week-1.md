# WEEK 1 – Notebook Notes

## 1. Artificial Intelligence
AI is a field of Computer Science that develops systems capable of reasoning, learning, perception, planning, and decision-making.

Main capabilities:
- Perception
- Reasoning
- Learning
- Planning
- Decision Making
- Problem Solving

## 2. AI Hierarchy
- AI: The broad field of intelligent systems.
- ML: A subset of AI that learns patterns from data.
- DL: A subset of ML based on deep neural networks.
AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL

> Not every AI system requires Machine Learning.

## 3. AI Approaches
Four perspectives:
1. Thinking Humanly
2. Acting Humanly
3. Thinking Rationally
4. Acting Rationally

## 4. Symbolic vs Data-Driven AI
Symbolic AI:
- Uses predefined rules and logic.
- Explicit knowledge.
- Easy to explain.
- Difficult to maintain many rules.
Data-Driven AI:
- Learns patterns from data.
- Uses training examples.
- Handles complex patterns.
- Depends on data quality.

## 5. Intelligent Agents
An agent perceives its environment and selects actions to achieve an objective.
- Sensors: Receive information.
- Actuators: Perform actions.
PEAS:
- P: Performance Measure
- E: Environment
- A: Actuators
- S: Sensors

## 6. Expert Systems
An AI system that uses expert knowledge and rules.

Components:
- Knowledge Base
- Inference Engine
- Working Memory
- User Interface
- Explanation Mechanism
Reasoning:
- Forward Chaining: Facts → Conclusion.
- Backward Chaining: Goal → Required Facts.

## 7. Machine Learning
Training: Learning parameters from data.

Inference: Using a trained model to make predictions.

## 8. AI History
- 1956: Dartmouth workshop.
- 1970s–1980s: Expert Systems.
- 1990s: Statistical ML.
- 2010s: Deep Learning.
- Modern AI: Transformers and Foundation Models.

## 9. Important Concepts
- Narrow AI: Task-specific intelligence.
- AGI: Hypothetical general intelligence.
- Turing Test: Evaluates human-like communication.
- XOR: Not linearly separable.
- AI Winters: Periods of reduced AI investment and enthusiasm.
